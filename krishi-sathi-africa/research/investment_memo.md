## 2. Answers to the 18 Mandatory Investment Committee Questions

### Q1: Is the opportunity sufficiently evidenced to justify further investment of time and resources?
**YES, subject to the B2B2C pivot.** Sub-Saharan Africa hosts over 33 million smallholders facing acute climate-induced crop yield collapses. The combination of open continental 30m digital soil mapping (iSDAsoil) and structured cooperative distribution makes an unconflicted decision engine technically and commercially viable without capital-intensive hardware.

### Q2: Which country should be investigated first?
**TANZANIA (United Republic of Tanzania).** Selected over Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, and Rwanda via our weighted scoring model (Score: 4.20/5.00). Tanzania combines a massive agricultural GDP share (27%), 6 million smallholder households, 100% Swahili linguistic unity, stable macroeconomic governance, and **the absence of an entrenched venture-backed digital advisory monopoly** (such as Safaricom DigiFarm in Kenya).

### Q3: Which specific region or agricultural zone should be prioritized?
**THE SOUTHERN HIGHLANDS (specifically Mbeya, Songwe, and Iringa regions).** The breadbasket of Tanzania, producing >46% of national maize. Features dependable unimodal rainfall (800–1,400mm), acute soil acidity constraints ($pH < 5.2$), anchor research presence (**TARI Uyole Centre**), and over 450 organized AMCOS cooperatives within the SAGCOT agricultural corridor.

### Q4: Which farmer segment should be targeted?
**Commercializing smallholder farming households** cultivating between 1.5 and 3.5 acres (0.6 to 1.4 hectares), organized in registered AMCOS cooperatives, currently trapped in continuous low-margin maize monoculture.

### Q5: Which initial crop or crop group should be addressed?
A five-crop portfolio centered on **cereal-legume diversification**:
1. **Baseline / Food Security:** Maize (*Zea mays*).
2. **Immediate Cash & Soil Nitrogen:** Common Bean (*Phaseolus vulgaris*, certified varieties Uyole 03 / Uyole 16).
3. **Institutional Off-Take:** Soybean (*Glycine max*, inoculated).
4. **Drought Hedging:** Sunflower (*Helianthus annuus*).
5. **Highland Cash Crop:** Irish Potato (*Solanum tuberosum*).

### Q6: What is the exact problem worth solving?
**The "Continuous Maize Monoculture Trap."** Smallholders deplete soil nitrogen and acidify their land with repetitive maize farming, paying exorbitant fertilizer prices (Urea/DAP > 45% of OpEx) only to sell into volatile harvest gluts at depressed margins (~$123/acre). They lack plot-specific agronomic and economic confidence to safely reallocate 30%–50% of acreage to high-margin legumes (~$258/acre) without risking household food supplies.

### Q7: What existing alternatives are farmers using?
1. *Local Agro-Dealers (Maduka ya Pembejeo):* Conflicted verbal advice designed to clear high-margin chemical inventory.
2. *Informal Peer Networks / Radio:* Generalized, non-localized agronomic advice.
3. *Single-Crop Programs:* Public tools like AKILIMO (strictly limited to cassava).

### Q8: What credible evidence demonstrates unmet demand?
Field evaluations by TARI Uyole and the World Bank show smallholders who adopt certified legume seeds achieve a **100%+ increase in gross margin per acre (TZS 670,347 vs TZS 320,000 for maize)**. Cooperatives demand crop scheduling tools to reduce loan defaults and expand aggregated produce volumes.

### Q9: Which competitors are already operating?
- *Regional Giants:* Apollo Agriculture, Safaricom DigiFarm, One Acre Fund (all heavily concentrated in Kenya; zero presence in Tanzania's Southern Highlands).
- *Research Platforms:* AKILIMO (cassava only), Precision Development (PxD - lightweight SMS push, donor-funded).
- *Hardware Testing:* AgroCares (NIR scanner - capped by \$3,000 device cost).

### Q10: What differentiation can Krishi Sathi realistically establish?
1. **Zero-Friction Spatial Ingestion:** Automated 30m soil inference via iSDAsoil (no lab test required from farmer).
2. **Unconflicted Advice:** No financial incentive to sell specific chemical brands.
3. **Household Economic Optimization:** Guarantees domestic food security acreage while maximizing net cash margin.
4. **Native Swahili Localized Engine:** Integrated directly into AMCOS cooperative extension workflows.

### Q11: What data is available, what is missing, and what must be collected locally?
- *Available (Open Tier 1/2):* iSDAsoil 30m (soil nutrients/pH), CHIRPS (precipitation), NASA POWER (climate/evapotranspiration), FAO GAEZ v4 (crop envelopes).
- *Missing / Must Collect Locally:* Ward-level agro-dealer seed inventory tracking, local farmgate price differentials, and TARI Uyole regional variety performance benchmarks.

### Q12: Which technical approach is appropriate?
A **Four-Stage Hybrid Constraint-Optimization Engine**:
1. *Deterministic Hard Rules:* Physiological survival exclusion (soil pH, elevation, slope).
2. *Calibrated Machine Learning:* Geospatial suitability scoring (LightGBM on local climate/soil features).
3. *Linear Optimization:* Household acreage allocation balancing food security vs. profit.
4. *Swahili Explanation Layer:* Plain-language reasoning with safety/abstention guardrails.

### Q13: Who should pay and why?
**Agricultural Marketing Co-operative Societies (AMCOS) and Commercial Off-Takers via B2B2C Licensing.** Billed at **\$1.50 to \$2.00 per farmer per season** (or \$1,200/year per 1,000-member cooperative). AMCOS recovers \$8–\$12 in incremental marketing cess per member from increased legume yields and reduced credit defaults, yielding an immediate 4x–6x institutional ROI.

### Q14: What pilot should be conducted first?
A **Controlled 500-Farmer Field Pilot** across 3 AMCOS cooperatives in the Mbeya/Songwe rural corridor over one full *Masika* cropping season (November 2026 to June 2027), testing a 60% Maize / 40% Common Bean diversification plan against a control group.

### Q15: What budget, partners and capabilities are required?
- *Budget:* **\$75,000 Seed Phase Allocation** (covering 9 months of pilot operations, cloud APIs, and field enumeration).
- *Partners:* TARI Uyole (scientific MoU), SAGCOT Centre, 3 selected AMCOS boards, and a licensed local seed distributor (e.g., Tanseed International).
- *Core Capabilities:* Full-stack geospatial engineer, Swahili agricultural extension lead, and an agricultural economist.

### Q16: What evidence would justify scaling?
1. $\ge 65\%$ farmer adoption rate of recommended acreage allocations in pilot co-ops.
2. Verified $\ge 30\%$ increase in net household seasonal farm income compared to control group.
3. $\ge 2$ commercial AMCOS boards willingly renewing enterprise software contracts for Year 2 out of their own marketing revenue.

### Q17: What findings would invalidate the opportunity?
1. AMCOS cooperative boards refuse to pay a \$1.50 software fee per member.
2. Local agro-dealers suffer systemic stock-outs of certified legume seeds, preventing execution.
3. Ground-truth wet chemistry tests reveal >35% error in iSDAsoil pH predictions in Southern Highland volcanic soils.
